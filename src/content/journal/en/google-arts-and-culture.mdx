The digital possibilities for museums and exhibitions are constantly increasing and offer exhibition teams various opportunities to present content, collections and stories not only in the physical space, but also online worldwide. And with great creative freedom!

Google Arts and Culture is a central platform for this form of digital mediation.

In the following practical guide, I will show you how to make content digitally accessible with Google Arts and Culture. You will receive a sound introduction to the platform and learn how to implement storytelling in digital exhibitions using practical examples.

  

 
  
  

## What is Google Arts and Culture?

Google Arts and Culture is a free platform of the Google Cultural Institute, which was launched in 2011 to make cultural heritage digitally accessible. Since then, over 2000 partner institutions worldwide have been integrated - including museums (e.g. the Guggenheim in Bilbao or the MoMA in New York), archives, galleries, foundations and universities.

Google Arts and Culture offers exhibition teams the opportunity to create their own exhibitions in the digital space, regardless of physical space boundaries, opening hours or geographical location. The platform provides tools with which high-quality content such as images, videos, 3D objects, texts and audio formats can be published.

Publishing takes place via an intuitive editorial system that allows content to be maintained independently - from themed series and biographies to complex storylines. For many museums, the platform represents an extension of traditional exhibition formats in order to reach a broader target group.

## Why Google Arts and Culture is an opportunity for museums

Digital presence is now a central component of museum work. Google Arts and Culture makes it possible to make content accessible online to a wide audience in a targeted, narrative and visually appealing way. For small and medium-sized museums in particular, the platform offers the potential to become digitally visible.

### Advantages of the platform for museums

The platform is characterized by a combination of exhibition teams, creative freedom and technological support. Curators can structure their content flexibly, set thematic focuses and decide for themselves how the story of an object or collection is told.

**Important advantages at a glance:**

- **Free use:** No license or hosting fees for partner institutions

- **Intuitive tools:** Simple preparation of texts, images, audio and video formats, even the creation of 360° virtual tours is possible

- **Editorial control:** Content design is the responsibility of the museum team

- **Digital archiving:** Content is accessible and searchable in the long term

### Accessibility for new target groups

A key advantage of Google Arts and Culture is its ability to break down barriers. For people who are unable to visit museums for geographical, physical or financial reasons, the platform offers low-threshold access to art, culture and history.

**Target groups that can be reached by Google Arts and Culture:**

- Pupils and students worldwide

- People interested in culture in rural regions

- People with reduced mobility

### Global reach and visibility

With the publication on Google Arts and Culture, the museum becomes part of a worldwide network of cultural institutions. In this way, smaller museums also get attention on the global stage.

Google Arts and Culture can also be used to find specific thematic collections and curated collections (e.g. "Climate change and society").

## From conception to exhibition on Google Arts and Culture

Preparing content digitally follows its own rules compared to traditional preparation. A well-founded content and strategic concept is required in order to target the content to the audience and select the different media formats. It is important that the digital exhibition is not simply a copy of the physical exhibition, but represents an independent form of storytelling and communication.

### From application to exhibition: the Google Arts onboarding process for museums

Access to the platform is via a partnership with Google by means of an application process. Once accepted as a partner institution, museums receive access to the platform's content management system (CMS). Both this process and the partnership are free of charge, but are subject to certain quality and content expectations. If you are planning a new digital exhibition for publication on the platform, you should contact the Google team. You will then be assigned a contact person for your project and can discuss the next steps with your contact person in a video call. Technical questions can also be clarified here. In any case, find out how many slides can currently be created for an exhibition and how many characters including spaces the texts may contain, as experience has shown that the criteria can change here. More information is available directly from Google Arts & Culture on this page.

### Target group orientation and concept

Once there are no more obstacles to joining the platform, the next step is to design the exhibition. Who is the digital exhibition primarily aimed at? Which interests, age groups or level of prior knowledge are addressed? Should visitors be addressed worldwide or nationwide?

Based on these considerations, the narrative style, scope and use of media can be better planned.

### Selection of formats and content

Once the conceptual basis is in place, the next step is to select suitable media formats and content types. Google Arts and Culture supports a variety of digital elements that exhibition teams can use to make their content lively and varied.

#### **Formats: Images, texts, videos, 3D objects**

Different formats can be combined depending on the collection and theme:

- **Images:** High-resolution object photographs, detail shots, archive material

- **Texts:** Introductions, captions, essays, articles

- **Videos:** Interviews, making-of material, thematic short films

- **Virtual tours:** of rooms or similar.

#### **Contents: Biographies, hero's journeys, facts**

Digital exhibitions on Google Arts and Culture can also be presented in an intellectual, sophisticated and emotionally accessible way using a variety of narrative approaches. This not only appeals to a broad audience, but also creates a personal connection to the content.

Examples of narrative approaches:

- **Biographies:** Life stories of historical or contemporary personalities

- **Hero's Journey:** Dramaturgically structured narrative with turning points and the development of a particular character

- **Facts and contexts:** Historical backgrounds, cultural-scientific classifications or data visualizations

## Why storytelling is ideal for Google Arts and Culture: 2 practical examples

Digital exhibitions not only thrive on high-quality media, but above all on a clear, emotionally accessible narrative. Unlike in the physical space, where visitors can navigate freely through rooms and be inspired, the digital space requires more direction from the curation.

Storytelling offers exhibition teams the opportunity to present complex topics using personal perspectives, suspenseful arcs and emotional anchors. Especially on Google Arts and Culture, where content is often consumed on the move or on the fly, a good story is crucial for attention and dwell time.

### Practical example: The Frank family - telling a family story

The exhibition "[The History of the Frank Family](https://g.co/arts/3Fo6Uiz3w3DjrT2Y6)" (Jewish Museum Frankfurt & Anne Frank Fonds Basel) on Google Arts and Culture traces Anne Frank's family history since the 17th century.

The curatorial approach follows a chronological structure. This begins with the family's roots in Frankfurt am Main and Landau an der Pfalz, through life in the German Empire and the Weimar Republic to the post-war period. Historically important stages are linked to the family's life.

Central components of the digital exhibition are postcards, photographs and memorabilia from several generations as well as personal letters and reports documenting family cohesion and everyday life.

A key storytelling element of this digital exhibition is the use of original sounds:

- Letters from Alice Frank, Anne Frank's grandmother, were not only shown as visual documents, but also made accessible acoustically. Visitors can listen to excerpts - a sensual, immersive experience that creates closeness and authenticity.

- The exhibition concludes with an interview with Buddy Elias (1925-2015), cousin of Anne Frank and long-time president of the Anne Frank Fonds. His personal retrospective lends the exhibition a strong emotional resonance and a contemporary relevance.

This example shows how personal stories can be conveyed in an exciting and tangible way using multimedia storytelling. This not only makes it possible to reach a broad audience in a low-threshold way, but also to present the multi-layered family history in a differentiated and respectful way.

### Practical example: Arno Lustiger - telling a biography using the "hero's journey" storytelling method

Arno Lustiger (1924-2012) is a survivor of National Socialism, historian and Jewish activist. By presenting his biography in a structured way using the storytelling method of the hero's journey, which shows how a person overcomes challenges and rises above themselves, visitors are guided through his life story emotionally and based on facts. This makes his work tangible.

To mark the 100th anniversary of his birth, the exhibition at the Jewish Museum Frankfurt highlights the life and work of Arno Lustiger through 5 thematic stations:

- **Living on:** The years after liberation

- **Building up:** His role in the reconstruction of Jewish life

- **Helping to shape:** Involvement in culture, business and society

- **Enlighten:** His voice as a publicist and historian

- **Remember:** The estate and his public work until the end

The exhibition combines biographical milestones with a social context and also focuses on unexpected aspects - such as Lustiger's success as a fashion entrepreneur or his work as an art collector. The result is a multi-layered portrait that goes beyond mere biographical data and links the history of his impact with his personal development.

Since [May 2024, the exhibition can be found on Google Arts and Culture](https://g.co/arts/TfECWpohyEhaYyVw5) and offers visitors an appealing visual variety through the different object categories, which can convey information and transport emotions:

- Photographs from different phases of life

- Letters and newspaper articles documenting political and personal positions

- Posters and paintings that provide insights into his cultural network

- Videos, including excerpts from contemporary witnesses and lectures

The visual and content diversity as well as the content in simple language allow visitors low-threshold access and are therefore also aimed at an international and non-academic audience. [Read more about my involvement in the exhibition here](/digital-storytelling)

## Frequently asked questions about Google Arts and Culture

### **How can museums participate in Google Arts and Culture?

Museums can apply directly to Google Arts and Culture. Google supports institutions worldwide in digitizing their collections and curating exhibitions online.

### **What advantages does Google Arts and Culture offer for smaller museums?

Smaller museums also benefit from the platform, as they can reach an international audience without high investments. Google provides technical support and tools.

### **Are there any costs for the use of Google Arts and Culture?

Participation in Google Arts and Culture is free of charge for museums. However, resources should be planned for the digitization and content preparation of the exhibitions.

### **How important is storytelling for a successful exhibition on Google Arts and Culture?

Storytelling is crucial for reaching digital visitors emotionally and guiding them through the digital exhibition. A clear central theme increases the length of stay and the impact.

### **Can museums also publish interactive content on Google Arts and Culture?

Yes, Google Arts and Culture supports a variety of formats, including virtual tours, 3D models, videos and interactive elements that enrich the user experience.

### **What criteria should museums consider when creating an exhibition with Google Arts and Culture?

A digital exhibition at Google Arts and Culture may currently comprise up to 30 slides, although shorter formats of 20 slides are usually recommended. A slide is a single page within the digital exhibition, for which Google offers various format templates from which museums can choose. Each slide can contain a maximum of 280 characters including spaces plus the title. However, longer texts can also be stored in the object database under the individual view page for the objects. This is recommended in any case, as individual objects can also be linked in social media posts, for example to create a feature on a specific topic.

### **Can museums change their digital exhibition again after publication?

  

Yes, it is possible to change the texts and images or add further content to the digital exhibition. However, the above-mentioned restrictions with regard to the number of slides must be observed.
