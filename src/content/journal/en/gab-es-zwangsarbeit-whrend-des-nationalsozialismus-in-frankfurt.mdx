## Did they also exist in Frankfurt during National Socialism?

There are currently around 600 references to places in the Frankfurt city area where forced laborers were quartered or employed. These are based on eyewitness accounts and research by various institutions since the 1990s. In the case of foreign civilian workers (the term "foreign workers" is often used)** **they were civilians from Western and Eastern European countries that had been occupied by the Wehrmacht. They were recruited, enlisted or deported as workers. They worked for private companies and authorities, for farmers and families, who also supervised them. Even those who had originally come voluntarily were not allowed to leave their jobs, so they lived and worked under duress and usually under degrading conditions. Around a third of the civilian workforce were women and many minors were also forced to perform forced labor. In the summer of 1944 alone, 5.7 million people were working as civilian laborers in the German Reich.

## Where can I find information on the forced labor of foreign civilian workers in Frankfurt am Main?

A good starting point for researching places of forced labor under National Socialism in Frankfurt is the website [Frankfurt 1933-1945](https://www.frankfurt1933-1945.de/home). It provides information for research and summarizes the latest state of research from 2003. Under the heading **Economy and Work/** **Help for Research** you will find the [Findbuch erzählter Frankfurter Geschichte](https://www.frankfurt1933-1945.de/beitraege/hilfe-zur-recherche-4/beitrag/findbuch-erzaehlter-frankfurter-geschichte-audio-beitraege-mit-zeitzeugen-und-zeitzeuginnen), which also lists interviews with former forced laborers. One of the most important sources, however, are the household registers, which were compiled by the police stations from 1929 to 1945. Although the records of some districts (Innenstadt, Sachsenhausen, Niederrad, Schwanheim, Griesheim and Höchst etc.) have not survived due to the war, around [2,800 household registers](https://www.frankfurt1933-1945.de/beitraege/zwangsarbeit/beitrag/die-hausstandsbuecher-als-quelle-fuer-die-zwangsarbeit-in-frankfurt) have been preserved for the other districts. A complete overview of the sources used by the Institut für Stadtgeschichte for its research can be found in [this essay](https://www.frankfurt1933-1945.de/beitraege/zwangsarbeit/beitrag/tabellarische-uebersicht-der-firmen-institutionen-und-behoerden-die-zwangs-und-fremdarbeiter-beschaeftigt-haben).

The archive also provides two tables on its website that provide an overview of the companies, institutions and authorities that benefited from forced labor. [Table 1](https://www.frankfurt1933-1945.de/fileadmin/user_upload/table/Tabelle_Zwangsarbeiter.pdf) / [Table 2](https://www.frankfurt1933-1945.de/beitraege/zwangsarbeit/beitrag/tabellarische-uebersicht-der-sonstigen-lager-fuer-fremd-und-zwangsarbeiter) In addition to the places of deployment, the places of accommodation are usually also documented. Further information can also be found on the website of the [Landesgeschichtliches Informationssystem](https://www.lagis-hessen.de/de/subjects/index/sn/nstopo)s. Further information can also be found in the archive holdings of the Arolsen Archives.

For my research, I also took a look at the state of research in the 1980s and 1990s and compared this with the state of research at the Institute for Urban History. Important sources here included the Hessian state government's response to a question from the Green parliamentary group in 1984 and historical-scientific publications such as the 'Heimatgeschichtliche Wegweiser' by Ursula Krause-Schmitt and others. Ultimately, the Frankfurt History App mainly included those places for which - through comparison with archive sources and research results from institutions such as the Institute of City History - a direct connection with the system of forced labor could be proven. Accordingly, there are still numerous sources that need to be evaluated in the future in order to record and investigate further places of forced labor. I will now introduce you to the sources I have used and explain how you can research forced labor in Frankfurt yourself.

### Explanation of the most important sources on forced labor in Frankfurt am Main

The "Labour Deployment" department of the **German Labour Front** (DAF), whose headquarters for the Hesse-Nassau region was located at Wilhelm-Leuschner-Straße 69, recorded which companies used forced labourers during the Second World War and also organized the supervision of the forced labour camps in Frankfurt. Around 25,000 forced laborers and prisoners of war were permanently deployed in Frankfurt companies, public authorities and private households during the war years. In total, more than 49,000 people were forced to perform forced labor in Frankfurt am Main between 1939 and 1945. The actual number was much higher, but as many household registers were destroyed during the war, figures on forced labor are not available for all districts of Frankfurt. The DAF files can be found in the **Hessisches Hauptstaatsarchiv Wiesbaden** (fonds 483, call numbers 705, 773, 902, 3259, 7328 and fonds 480, call number 6522a).

In 1946, the **United Nations Relief and Rehabilitation Administration** (UNRRA) launched a so-called alien search operation. UNRRA was responsible for registering people from UN member states, looking after them and helping them to return to their home countries. The aim of this operation was to clarify the whereabouts of millions of displaced persons. The individual police stations in Frankfurt am Main reported to UNRRA how many forced laborers were working and living in their district. The digital database of the **Arolsen Archives** is ideal for researching forced labor in Germany.

In the archive tree you will find the collection as follows: 2. registrations of foreigners and German persecutees by public institutions, insurance companies and firms (1939 - 1947) >> 2.2. documents on registrations of foreigners and the deployment of forced laborers, 1939 - 1945 >> 2.2.0. forced labor ("deployment of foreign ethnic workers") >> 2.2.0.1 correspondence and documents on forced labor >> [**Information on various places of detention, labor camps, prisoner-of-war camps and other camps in the district - 1st SK Frankfurt**](https://collections.arolsen-archives.org/de/archive/2-2-0-1_9067800).

At the top right of the documents you will find information about the country from which the forced laborers came. This is followed by an excerpt from a table that lists the companies where they were employed and the number of people who were forced to work. In some cases, there is also information about what happened to the people.

Forced laborers were often not housed in the companies in which they were deployed. It was therefore important to distinguish between forced labor factories and forced labor camps. In addition, the research in the aforementioned collections once again made clear the extent to which forced laborers were exploited in Frankfurt and the surrounding area. The interactive map of the **Frankfurt History App** makes the extent of the camp system in Frankfurt visible.

              

              

            
          
        

      

    

  

 
  
  

#### How can I research the biographies of forced laborers?

It is generally advisable to use the [digital collection on forced labor](https://collections.arolsen-archives.org/de/archive/2-2-0-1) under National Socialism at the Arolsen Archives. Using a case study, I will show you how you can reconstruct a biography using the Arolsen Archives database.

We start with the information provided by the table "[Forced laborers](https://www.frankfurt1933-1945.de/fileadmin/user_upload/table/Tabelle_Zwangsarbeiter.pdf)" from the Institute for City History Frankfurt am Main on the role of the Deutsche Reichsbahn: This was based at Frankfurt Central Station and operated 22 forced labor camps in the city. One of the camps was located in the dance hall of the Stapf restaurant at Oeserstraße 16. The forced laborers who were housed there worked for Gleisbautrupps 6/Eilgutabfertigung. The Arolsen Archives database contains the holdings of the Deutsche Reichsbahn company health insurance fund, which contains over 4,700 index cards with detailed information on the workers who were deployed in Frankfurt am Main.

In the archive tree you will find the collection as follows: 2. registrations of foreigners and German persecutees by public institutions, insurance companies and firms (1939 - 1947) >> 2.2 Documents on registrations of foreigners and the use of forced laborers, 1939 - 1945 >> 2.2.2 Various authorities and firms (individual-related documents) >> 2.2.2.1 Wartime card index (registration and registration cards, work books, individual correspondence) >> [Member cards of the company health insurance fund of the Deutsche Reichsbahn, Reichsbahnausbesserungswerk Frankfurt am Main (shelfmark 10007395).](https://collections.arolsen-archives.org/de/archive/2-2-2-1_10007395)

The following people listed in this inventory worked for track construction team 6 (random sample):

Giovanni AMENDOLA, signature on his index card: 76432886

Remo D ANTONIO, signature of his index card: 76432910

Matteo BALDUZZI, signature on his index card: 76432969

Irma ANGIOLI, signature on her file card: 76432901

Using the "Name search" function in the Arolsen Archives database, you can now often find further documents on people from other collections under the personal names (for instructions, see the blog article "Historical address books in research on biographies, families and places").
