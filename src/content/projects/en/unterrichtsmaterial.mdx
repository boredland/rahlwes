As a freelance historian, I combine historical research with contemporary didactics: I develop well-founded teaching and learning materials as well as handouts and accompanying media for museums, memorial sites, foundations and educational institutions. The material helps pupils to deepen their knowledge of cultural-historical topics using the example of biographies, exhibits and documents.

## History education for museums, memorials, foundations and educational institutions

Good educational material thrives on exciting historical sources, innovative approaches and multi-perspective contextualization. As a historian who works at the interface between research, museums and education, I include not only historical documents, photos and posters in the teaching material, but also eyewitness accounts and objects from museum collections. In addition, I make sure that the concepts are designed to offer differentiated educational opportunities that can be flexibly adapted to the learning level of the respective group.

- **Development of teaching materials:** Conception of worksheets, learning booklets and tasks (Sek. I & II) based on historical research.
- Handouts for teachers:** Creation of tailor-made accompanying materials for the preparation and follow-up of visits to exhibitions, museums or memorial sites.
- Accompanying materials for multimedia formats:** Development of tailor-made didactic concepts and handouts for podcasts, short films, apps or digital exhibitions.

## Selected references & projects

<TwoColumn heading="Educational material on Jewish life, history and culture in Hesse" src="/uploads/unterrichtsmaterial-dialog-4.png" alt="Illustration by Pauline Wernig on digital cultural mediation and storytelling in the museum for the historian Ann-Kathrin Rahlwes.">

On behalf of the Hessian State Center for Political Education, I created **accompanying teaching materials** for historical-political education in schools for the podcast series "[Jewish Life, History and Culture in Hesse](https://hlz.hessen.de/themen/podcast/juedisches-leben-geschichte-und-kultur-in-hessen/)" and was involved in the conception of episodes 10 to 12.

Jewish life has a long and varied tradition in Hesse. This is precisely where the educational material comes in. It offers teachers a low-threshold, multimedia introduction to topics relating to Jewish history, culture and the present in the region from the past to the present.

The starting point for each material unit is a podcast episode. The radio play-style introduction and the subsequent expert discussions with guests can be used as an interactive stimulus in the classroom. The supplementary teaching materials pick up on the main content of the respective episode and offer methods for in-depth study.

### Who is the educational material suitable for?

The material is suitable for teaching in the subjects of religion or ethics as well as history from year 9 up to upper secondary school as well as for extracurricular learning groups and university teaching. It can be used across subjects, for example in history, political education, ethics or German.

### Educational materials that I have created:

#### Moritz Daniel Oppenheim (episode 8)

[**To the teaching material**](https://hlz.hessen.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Podcast/Juedisches_leben/Unterrichtsmaterial_zur_Folge_8_Moritz_Daniel_Oppenheim.pdf)

The didactic aim of this teaching unit is to promote an understanding of Jewish culture, identities and traditions. The starting point is an examination of the life and work of the painter Moritz Daniel Oppenheim. Jewish emancipation in Germany, the diversity within the Jewish community and its cultural contributions are addressed using selected paintings and biographical events.

#### Community life in Hanau and Offenbach around a new Torah (episode 9)

[**To the teaching material**](https://hlz.hessen.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Podcast/Juedisches_leben/Unterrichtsmaterial_Folge_9_Eine_Torah_zieht_ein.pdf)

The teaching material for podcast episode 9 "A Torah moves in - anniversaries in the Jewish communities of Hanau and Offenbach" sheds light on the central importance of the Torah in Jewish community life. At the same time, the new beginning of Jewish communities after 1945 is discussed using the examples of Offenbach and Hanau. In April 2025, the Jewish community of Hanau celebrated the 20th anniversary of its re-establishment after the Shoah.

#### Maccabi and Jewish unity (episode 10)

**To the teaching material**

The teaching material for podcast episode 10 "Maccabi and the Jewish Eintracht" sheds light on the Jewish history of sport in Hesse using the example of two clubs: Maccabi Frankfurt and Eintracht Frankfurt. Based on their club histories, pupils can understand how Jewish life has shaped sporting and social life in Frankfurt since the late 19th century

<TwoColumn heading="“The family was Jewish ...” Teaching material on a German-Jewish family history" src="/uploads/unterrichtsmaterial-design-ohne-titel.png" alt="An orange colored pencil. This is about writing down history.">

On behalf of the Fritz Bauer Institute in Frankfurt am Main, I developed a [brochure](https://www.fritz-bauer-institut.de/fileadmin/editorial/publikationen/paedagogik/unterrichtsmodule/UM-07_Familie.pdf) for teaching at upper secondary level at grammar schools, secondary modern schools and lower secondary schools. The material booklet "*Die Familie war jüdisch* ... Biografisches Lernen am Beispiel einer deutsch-jüdischen Familie" was published in September 2024. It is based on a [short film](https://thefamilywasjewish.com/de/die-familie-war-judisch/) of the same name by Judy Rosenthal, which was produced in 2021 as part of the exhibition "Frankfurt im NS - eine Stadt macht mit" at the Historisches Museum Frankfurt. In it, Judy Rosenthal searches for traces of her family members and their lives in Munich during the Weimar Republic and National Socialism.

For example, the biography of her great-grandmother Dorline Springer is presented. She came from a family that had run a textile store in the center of Munich since 1865. She gave up the store in 1932, but continued to work in the successor business. Her daughter Anny emigrated to the USA (Chicago), which she also visited in 1937. However, it was with a heavy heart that she decided to return to Munich. In August 1939, Dorline was forced to leave her home and had to move into a so-called ghetto house. After she was deported from Munich to the Piaski ghetto together with 989 other Jews at the beginning of April 1942, all trace of her was lost.

</TwoColumn>

<TwoColumn src="/uploads/unterrichtsmaterial-kein-titel-1080-x-1350-px.png" alt="View of the material booklet “The family was Jewish ... Biographical learning using the example of a German-Jewish family”, Fritz Bauer Institute Frankfurt am Main." imageSide="right">

The brochure presents a total of six biographies of Judy Rosenthal's relatives: In addition to Dorline Springer and her daughter Anny Rosenthal, also the biography of her great-aunt Elisabeth Springer, of Anny's husband, Julius Rosenthal. and of her cousin Gertrud Farnbacher and her cousins Ernst and Rudolf Farnbacher. These biographies can be used in the classroom to deal with various topics relating to the history of Jewish life in Germany - such as Jewish life before 1933, "Gleichschaltung" 1933-34, the Nuremberg Laws and the November 1938 progroms. The brochure offers a variety of ways to incorporate the material into the classroom. For example, the thematic unit can be introduced using the cluster or flashlight method. Here, for example, questions are asked about how the young people themselves remember important moments in their family history and why people are concerned with their family history.

The brochure also contains some fun tools, such as a quiz on an advertising stamp from the Springer family's store from the turn of the century. In addition, the brochure also provides a glossary so that topics such as "Kindertransporte" can be explored in greater depth. The teaching material concludes with a unit on the culture of remembrance and invites students to explore forms of commemoration using the example of the [orphan carousel](https://www.kunst-im-oeffentlichen-raum-frankfurt.de/de/page134.html?id=455).

<TwoColumn heading="Teaching material on Anne Frank's family history" src="/uploads/unterrichtsmaterial-anka-010.jpg" alt="Teaching material on Anne Frank and the history of her family and ancestors">

The [material booklet](https://www.bs-anne-frank.de/mediathek/publikationen/freiheitsrechte) " Freiheitsrechte. The example of Anne Frank's ancestors and family" presents teaching material on the history of "civil liberties" using the example of Anne Frank's ancestors and family. The term "freedom rights" refers here to the civil rights and legal concepts of the 19th century up to the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in 1948 and its reception today. Brief introductions to the following rights are presented in line with the issues young people face in their everyday lives: The right to freedom of travel, the right to freely choose one's place of residence, the right to freely choose one's profession and the right to human rights.

I was responsible for the concept, historical research and text creation together with Ricarda Wawra. The project was realized in 2013. The project partner was Sandra Krawinkel (graphic design). The client was the Anne Frank Educational Center in cooperation with the Educational Center of the Fritz Bauer Institute and the Jewish Museum Frankfurt, Frankfurt am Main.

The chapters provide an insight into the everyday life of Anne Frank's ancestors and family from the perspective of the respective protagonists. Using original sources, quotes from letters and material suitable for young people, pupils are encouraged to consider the significance of the borders of political territories and the possibilities of overcoming them: Why do people embark on a journey? Why do people leave their homeland to start a new life in a foreign country? What is the significance of the (travel) passport?

This booklet provides an insight into the lives of young Jews since the French Revolution and illustrates their increasing self-confidence in exercising their civil rights and freedoms. This attitude endured despite all the political upheavals and catastrophes, as the last two chapters on applications for citizenship in Swiss emigration and the compensation file on Anne Frank show.

The booklet is suitable both for history lessons in 8th to 10th grade and as supplementary material to deepen the reading of Anne Frank's diary in German lessons. The work texts are self-contained units and are aimed at different learning levels, which are indicated in the table of contents. In addition, each chapter has a method kit: here you will find suggestions for lesson planning and examples of solutions.
